## 4. Obligations of a Data Fiduciary

"Applies to" is the firm when it decides why and how personal data is processed
(a Data Fiduciary, Act s.2(i)). All rows in this section start on DPDP-C03
(13 May 2027) unless stated.

### 4.3 Personal data breach

A "personal data breach" includes unauthorised processing and accidental
disclosure, loss of access, alteration or destruction (ACT s.2(u)).

| ID | Plain-English requirement | Applies to | Evidence needed | Source | Status | Scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DPDP-B01 | On becoming aware of a breach, tell each affected person without delay, in clear and plain words, through their account or registered contact: what happened (nature, extent, timing), likely consequences for them, what the firm is doing, what they can do to stay safe, and a contact who can answer questions. | Every Data Fiduciary | Breach response plan with a ready message template and contact list | ACT s.8(6); RULES r.7(1)(a)-(e) | VERIFIED | Yes |
| DPDP-B02 | On becoming aware of a breach, tell the Data Protection Board (a) without delay: a description including nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact; and (b) within 72 hours (or longer if the Board allows on written request): updated details, the facts and causes, mitigation, findings on who caused it, steps to prevent recurrence, and a report on the notices sent to affected people. | Every Data Fiduciary | Breach response plan with the two-step Board timeline and a named owner | ACT s.8(6); RULES r.7(2)(a), 7(2)(b)(i)-(vi) | VERIFIED | Yes |

### 4.4 Retention and erasure

| ID | Plain-English requirement | Applies to | Evidence needed | Source | Status | Scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DPDP-R01 | Erase personal data when consent is withdrawn or as soon as it is reasonable to assume the purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, unless a law requires it to be kept. Make processors erase it too. | Every Data Fiduciary | Retention schedule; deletion log; processor deletion confirmation | ACT s.8(7)(a), 8(7)(b) | VERIFIED | Yes |
| DPDP-R02 | Keep personal data, associated traffic data and logs of processing for at least one year from the date of processing (for the purposes in the Seventh Schedule), then erase unless another law requires longer retention. This also applies to processing done by processors. | Every Data Fiduciary | Retention schedule showing the one-year minimum; processor contract term | RULES r.8(3) and Illustration Case 2 | VERIFIED | Yes |
| DPDP-R03 | Fixed three-year inactivity erasure periods and the 48-hour pre-erasure warning apply only to very large e-commerce, online gaming and social media platforms. | Only the classes in the Third Schedule (not a law firm) | None for a law firm | RULES r.8(1), 8(2), Third Schedule | VERIFIED | No (info) |

Tension to flag for the lawyer: s.8(7) says erase when the purpose ends, while
r.8(3) says keep for at least one year. Other laws (for example professional
record-keeping, tax, limitation periods) may require longer retention; the Act
allows retention "necessary for compliance with any law".
